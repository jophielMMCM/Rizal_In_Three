THE ARREST

Rizal was arrested on July 6, 1892, shortly after returning to the Philippines from Hong Kong. His arrest came after he founded La Liga Filipina, an organization that promoted Filipino unity, mutual assistance, education, agriculture, commerce, and social reform (National Historical Commission of the Philippines [NHCP], 2015).

Although La Liga Filipina advocated peaceful reform, Spanish authorities viewed Rizal's activities and writings with suspicion. He was arrested on charges connected to his reform activities and was later deported to Dapitan, where he began his four-year exile (NHCP, 2000; National Commission for Culture and the Arts [NCCA], 2003).

Rizal's arrest demonstrated the Spanish colonial government's growing concern over Filipino reform movements and organizations. The authorities' decision to arrest and exile him also weakened La Liga Filipina, which was later reorganized by other Filipino reformists (NHCP, 2015; NCCA, 2003).
