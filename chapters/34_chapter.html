THE TRIAL

Rizal was tried by a military tribunal on charges of rebellion, sedition, and conspiracy in December 1896. The Spanish authorities accused him of supporting the Philippine Revolution through his writings and his association with reformist and revolutionary figures. During the trial, the prosecution presented witnesses and documents intended to connect Rizal to the revolutionary movement. Although Rizal defended himself and denied directly participating in the revolution, the military court found him guilty and sentenced him to death. The trial was widely criticized because Rizal was not given a full opportunity to defend himself against the accusations. His case reflected the Spanish colonial government's determination to punish him and suppress ideas associated with Filipino resistance and calls for reform.
